### Reading

Nothing found is an efferent to a sense organ in the strict sense. Three kinds of control exist:

1. **Central presynaptic inhibition of afferent terminals** (Dallmann 2025). It is in the connectome as
   synapses onto the afferents and the model already runs it, but as point-neuron synapses (N30 note:
   the inhibition lowers the afferent's spike rate rather than its terminal release; inferred).
2. **Neuromodulation of central sensory neurons by behavioural state** (octopamine on Mi4 and on
   lobula-plate cells in flight and walking). That is N19 (neuromodulator receptor maps) applied to
   visual cells, not a separate mechanism.
3. **Hunger acting on GRN terminals** (dopamine on labellar sugar GRNs, sNPF on ORNs). The model applies
   hunger to labellar GRN drive already. For the leg sugar lead (F-TASTE-LEG-1): the measured effect is a
   significant increase at 100 mM for labellar GRNs, and its size is not established here. On the morning of
   5 October a ×3 presynaptic gain on LgAG2 left MN9_L at 0 Hz and ×10 gave 3 Hz under m2 (DECISIONS
   04:40), so a hunger gain of the order Inagaki report would not rescue leg PER in the model on its
   own (inferred from the two results).

### Reading and the check run tonight

The one Drosophila whole-structure measurement is the leg compression. I used it as a held-out check of
B3's passive leg (DECISIONS 22:37 and its result, F-LEGK-1): the model's passive leg is 3-5× softer than
the living leg, with the compliance in the femur-tibia joint. In the living leg too the joint angles
change under load, so segment compliance is not what the experiment measures. The thorax spring has no
Drosophila measurement.

## Decisions (5 October)

- **B24 stays absent.** Its content splits into the connectome (presynaptic inhibition, limited by N30),
  N19 (state-dependent neuromodulation of central sensory cells) and the existing hunger gain. A
  separate switch would only relabel these. The data that would let N19 carry the visual part:
  octopamine receptor expression per optic-lobe cell type (single-cell transcriptomes of the optic lobe
  exist; not read tonight) and one measured gain change per type (Strother 2018 for Mi4).
- **B26 stays absent.** No Drosophila thorax or segment stiffness is measured; the wing generator stands
  in for thorax resonance in flight. Experiment that would settle it (for Ben's list): force-displacement
  of an excised adult Drosophila thorax at flight frequency, as Gau et al. did for bumblebee.

### Where B27 sits in the model (derived from the male-cns tables in `data/cache`, no token)

- **SpMNs are male-cns type ENXXX226** (inferred). 19 cells, one per hemineuromere T1-A6 with one extra
  in A6 (somaSide 10 R, 9 L), superclass `vnc_efferent`, predicted transmitter "unclear". The paper calls
  SpMNs unannotated efferents and gives "ENXXX226_T..." as its instance-label example; their top inputs
  in male-cns are INXXX204 (2,451 synapses), DNp24 (2,050), DNge137 (1,880), INXXX472 (1,729), DNpe040,
  DNpe043 and DNg27, which matches the paper's SpINA, SpINB, DNge137 and DNg27. SpINA's and SpINB's top
  outputs are ENXXX226 cells (SpINB: 295-359 synapses onto each of its top four).
- So the neural half of spiracle control is in the connectome and is simulated as neurons today
  (INXXX204, INXXX472, ENXXX226, DNg02). What is missing is the effector: ENXXX226 drives no body part,
  aperture does not exist, and nothing limits flight power by oxygen supply.

### Decision (5 October)

**B27 stays absent**, with its reason corrected: there is a measured behavioural coupling (spiracle
closure limits flight power within the flight bout; aperture tracks commanded flight power). Build plan,
when flight is a running milestone:

1. Spiracle aperture per segment as a body output driven by ENXXX226 rates (closing), first-order with a
   time constant to fit to the paper's activation traces (fitted).
2. Flight power ceiling as a function of thoracic aperture (fitted to the SpMN activation result; the
   oxygen and water budgets are not needed for a first version).
3. Held-out checks: DNg02 activation opens Sp1; SpINB activation opens Sp1 without changing wingbeat;
   SpMN silencing opens Sp1 (all read above). In the open-loop model these need SpMN baseline activity,
   which the model does not have at rest (all three are tonic-closure results), so they come after B27's
   output exists.

The heart has no identified behavioural coupling in the adult beyond the brain pulsation by the frontal
organ (unverified) and stays out of scope.

### Decision (5 October)

**S7 stays absent; the assumption is now stated precisely.** The model fly is a healthy, conventionally
reared laboratory fly. Microbiome and infection act on behaviour through pathways the model has as
other mechanisms: octopamine (N19 pools) for walking speed, sleep pressure (the organs and sleep
homeostat of `state:organs|model`) for infection-induced sleep. Validation data must record rearing
(conventional against axenic) and infection status as conditions, because axenic flies walk faster
(read above). No settling experiment is needed for the healthy-fly default.

### Decision (5 October)

**D1 stays absent** as a mechanism: no development model is built. Three facts are now recorded as the
specimen record, read, not modelled:

- **Age:** 5 days post-eclosion (mature by the courtship curve above). Validation data from flies far
  younger than 3 days or much older than a week should be flagged as a cross-age transfer.
- **Genotype:** Canton S G1 × w1118 F1 males (resolves the "not checked here" note on the sex and
  genotype row).
- **Rearing:** 12:12 light-dark, dissected at ZT 1.5. The model's clock (N26) starts at CT 0 by
  convention (`internal_state.State.z = 1`). Starting it at the specimen's ZT 1.5 is a small sourced
  change for a later switch; it only matters for runs that use the clock.

The ledger rows for age and rearing stay `absent`, because nothing in the model reads an age or a rearing
history. Their notes now carry the specimen values, so a development model or a validation join can use
them.

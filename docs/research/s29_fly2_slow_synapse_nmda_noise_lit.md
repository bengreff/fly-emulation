# Literature read: slow synaptic components, NMDA/Mg2+, and membrane-potential noise in adult Drosophila CNS

Search budget: ~40 min, four topics. All numbers below are from adult Drosophila melanogaster unless stated. "Read level" states exactly what I read (full text + figure/table number, vs. only an abstract/secondary summary produced by a search engine or a WebFetch model that did not have clean full text). Where I could not get clean full text (PDF layout failures, 403s), that is stated — those numbers are SECONDARY even when the underlying paper is a primary source, because I did not verify them against the actual figure/table myself.

## 1. GABA-B slow inhibition

**Wilson & Laurent 2005, J Neurosci 25(40):9069-9079** ("Role of GABAergic Inhibition in Shaping Odor-Evoked Spatiotemporal Patterns in the Drosophila Antennal Lobe"). Read as full text (PDF converted to text, UCSD course-reader mirror of the J Neurosci PDF; cross-checked against the Wilson lab's own PDF of the same article). Conditions: in vivo whole-cell patch clamp, antennal lobe PNs and LNs, Canton-S flies except where GAL4 lines noted, **all experimental animals adult females, 3-10 d post-eclosion**; recording temperature not stated in the Methods (likely room temperature — gap); saline (mM): 103 NaCl, 3 KCl, 5 TES, 10 trehalose, 10 glucose, 2 sucrose, 26 NaHCO3, 1 NaH2PO4, 1.5 CaCl2, **4 MgCl2**, 275 mOsm, pH ~7.3, bubbled 95% O2/5% CO2.

- GABA-gated hyperpolarization in PNs: picrotoxin (250 µM) blocks **46 ± 5% (n=14)** — this is the fast, ionotropic (GABA-A-type) fraction of the total GABA response amplitude. MEASURED, full text, Fig. 2B / Results text (p. 9072).
- The picrotoxin-resistant remainder of the PN GABA response (≈54%, the slow component) is blocked by CGP54626 (50 µM) by **97 ± 2% (n=5)**. MEASURED, full text, Fig. 2C / Results text.
- DERIVED (by me, simple subtraction, not stated as such in the paper): **slow (GABA-B)/fast (GABA-A) amplitude ratio in PNs ≈ 54/46 ≈ 1.17 : 1** (i.e. the CGP54626-sensitive component is slightly larger in peak IPSP amplitude than the picrotoxin-sensitive component, on average, under the microiontophoretic GABA-pulse protocol used — this is NOT an odor-evoked synaptic ratio, it is the ratio for a focal GABA puff).
- In LNs, picrotoxin alone blocks 98 ± 3% (n=6) of the GABA response, and CGP54626 has no further effect (101 ± 6% of control, n=5) — i.e. LNs show no detectable postsynaptic GABA-B component (MEASURED, full text, Fig. 2B/D).
- Kinetics: the text states only qualitatively that "the picrotoxin-resistant GABA conductance in PNs had slower kinetics than the picrotoxin-sensitive conductance (Fig. 2B,C, insets)." **No quantitative rise or decay time constant (tau) is given anywhere in the main text for either component** — this is a clear gap, not a number I could extract (I checked for "Table", "ms", "tau", "time constant" throughout the extracted text; none co-occur with a number for the GABA-B component). The only qualitative anchor is: "slow GABAB inhibitory epochs" contribute to odor-evoked inhibitory epochs lasting "hundreds of milliseconds" and CGP54626 affects PN spike rate specifically "1.5-2.5 s after stimulus onset," versus picrotoxin's effect being confined to "the first 500 ms" (MEASURED time windows, full text, Fig. 3C,D) — these are response-epoch windows, not fitted IPSP tau's.
- Mechanistic note the authors give (not a number): postsynaptic GABA-B receptors are thought (citing mammalian lit) to sit extrasynaptically, which is offered as *why* GABA-B kinetics look slower — this is the authors' interpretation, not a Drosophila measurement.

**Second, independent postsynaptic GABA-B measurement in a different cell type**: **not found** within the time budget. Candidates checked:
- Mushroom body / Kenyon cells: Amin, Grabe, Lin et al., "Localized inhibition in the Drosophila mushroom body," eLife 2020 (9:e56954) — read via WebFetch (full text access, not just abstract). This paper states KCs express GABA-B-R1 and GABA-B-R2 and that "APL inhibits KCs partly via GABA-B receptors" (citing Inada et al. 2017), but its own data are GCaMP6f calcium imaging plus local GABA puffs, not patch-clamp electrophysiology. **No amplitude ratio or decay tau is reported.** SECONDARY/qualitative only.
- Lobula plate tangential cells (HS/VS): Raghu & Borst-type anatomy papers (Raghu et al. 2007/2009, J Comp Neurol, "Synaptic organization of lobula plate tangential cells... GABA receptors and chemical release sites") show immunohistochemical/EM evidence that HS/VS dendrites receive GABAergic input and express GABA receptors, but I could not get full text in the time available (search snippets only — SECONDARY), and found no amplitude-ratio or tau number for GABA-B specifically in HS/VS.
- **Conclusion: Wilson & Laurent 2005's PN number (46±5% fast / ~54% slow) currently has no independent second measurement in hand from this search.** That is a real gap for falsification purposes, not a result to treat as corroborated.

**Presynaptic GABA-B (distinct mechanism, on ORN axon terminals, NOT postsynaptic)**:
- Olsen & Wilson 2008, Nature 452:956-960, "Lateral presynaptic inhibition mediates gain control in an olfactory circuit." SECONDARY read (WebSearch synthesis only; WebFetch of the Nature page was not attempted/blocked by time). Reports GABA-B receptors concentrated on ORN axon terminals and that GABA-B signaling scales presynaptic gain; GABA-A also contributes presynaptically. No numeric amplitude ratio captured from the secondary summaries available.
- Root et al. 2008, Neuron 59:311-321, "A Presynaptic Gain Control Mechanism Fine-Tunes Olfactory Behavior." SECONDARY read (WebSearch synthesis only). Reports GABA-B receptor (GABA-BR) expression on ORN terminals reduces ORN→PN synaptic gain; pheromone-sensing ORNs express high GABA-BR and show strong presynaptic inhibition; GABA-BR knockdown in specific ORNs impairs pheromone-dependent mate localization. No numeric values captured.
- **Both of these are presynaptic autoreceptor/heteroreceptor mechanisms on the ORN terminal, mechanistically and anatomically distinct from the postsynaptic GABA-B conductance Wilson & Laurent 2005 measure directly on the PN soma.** Do not merge the two numbers into one "GABA-B fraction" — they act on different sides of the ORN→PN synapse.

## 2. Muscarinic (mAChR-A/B) and mGluR (DmGluRA) slow components

**Bielopolski et al. 2019, eLife 8:e48264**, "Inhibitory muscarinic acetylcholine receptors enhance aversive olfactory learning in adult Drosophila." Read via WebFetch (full text, not abstract-only). Findings: mAChR-A knockdown increases calyx odor responses (Fig. 3); bath-applied muscarine decreases KC odor responses across compartments (Fig. 5); local muscarine application to the calyx suppresses odor responses (Fig. 6). All evidence is **two-photon GCaMP6f calcium imaging in vivo** (cuticle/trachea removed, brain superfused) — **no patch-clamp / voltage-or-current-clamp recording of an mAChR-A-mediated slow synaptic current or potential is reported anywhere in the paper.** No amplitude ratio to a fast nicotinic response, and no decay time constant in ms or s, is given. This is a load-bearing gap: the mechanism is inferred from Ca2+ imaging + genetics, not from a recorded slow synaptic conductance.

**Manoim et al. 2022, Current Biology 32:4438-4450.e5**, "Lateral axonal modulation is required for stimulus-specific olfactory conditioning in Drosophila" (mAChR-B at KC-KC axo-axonic synapses). SECONDARY read (WebSearch synthesis only — I did not get full text). Reported effects are again on **Ca2+ signals and dopamine-induced cAMP**, not a recorded synaptic current; behavioral readout is olfactory learning specificity. No amplitude ratio or tau located.

**DmGluRA, central recording**: I found no central-neuron (antennal lobe / mushroom body / etc.) patch-clamp recording of a DmGluRA-mediated synaptic current in the literature search. The one electrophysiological DmGluRA paper located, Bogdanik et al. 2004 J Neurosci 24(41):9105 ("The Drosophila Metabotropic Glutamate Receptor DmGluRA Regulates Activity-Dependent Synaptic Facilitation and Fine Synaptic Morphology"), is at the **larval neuromuscular junction**, not a central neuron, and reports (SECONDARY read, search synthesis) that DmGluRA mutants show **no change in basal EJC amplitude or paired-pulse facilitation** — i.e. no detectable fast/slow current ratio at baseline; the phenotype is activity-dependent facilitation under repetitive stimulation, not a resting slow synaptic component. Not a central-neuron, not adult, and not the quantity requested (amplitude ratio / tau) — flagged as off-target but the closest DmGluRA electrophysiology found.

**Overall for Topic 2: no quantitative amplitude-ratio or decay-tau number exists in hand for mAChR-A, mAChR-B, or DmGluRA in Drosophila central neurons.** All mechanistic claims in the literature I could reach are from calcium imaging or genetics, not recorded slow synaptic currents. This should be treated as an open gap, not approximated by analogy to GABA-B.

## 3. NMDA receptors and Mg2+ block

**Xia et al. 2005, Current Biology 15:603-615** ("NMDA Receptors Mediate Olfactory Learning and Memory in Drosophila"), PMC3045563. Read via WebFetch against the PMC mirror (fuller than abstract, but the fetch tool summarized rather than quoting the raw Methods/Results in full — treat the specific numbers below as full-text-figure level for the Mg2+/voltage claim, since it cites Fig. 2C explicitly, but not independently re-verified by me line-by-line).
- dNR1/dNR2-2 coexpressed in **Drosophila S2 cells**: voltage-dependent block by Mg2+ is seen; the figure (Fig. 2C) tests a concentration described in the fetched summary as "physiological Mg2+ (20 mM)" in that assay context (note: 20 mM is the concentration used in that expression-system assay, not a hemolymph-physiological concentration — distinguish from Topic 3's saline/hemolymph numbers below). MEASURED, full-text figure (Fig. 2C), but heterologous expression system (S2 cells), not a Drosophila central neuron.
- In **Xenopus oocytes**, the same dNR1/dNR2 combination shows **no voltage-dependent Mg2+ block up to 10 mM Mg2+** — i.e. Mg2+ sensitivity in this receptor combination is expression-system-dependent. MEASURED, full-text (same source).
- Site-directed finding: dNR1 carries an asparagine (N631) at the pore site homologous to vertebrate GluN1's Mg2+-block asparagine (N616); dNR2 carries a **glutamine (Q721)** at the homologous GluN2 site (vertebrate GluN2 has asparagine, N614) — this Q-for-N substitution is unusual among NMDARs and is part of why Mg2+ block in Drosophila NMDARs is weaker/context-dependent. MEASURED (sequence + mutagenesis), full text.
- **No IC50 or quantitative block constant at a stated holding voltage is reported** in what I could extract — this is a gap. **No decay time constant for an NMDA current is reported** in this paper — also a gap.
- This paper is heterologous-expression electrophysiology (S2 cells / oocytes), **not a recording from an intact Drosophila central neuron**. I found no recorded NMDA-mediated synaptic current (amplitude ratio to a fast AMPA/nicotinic-like component, or decay tau) in any adult or larval Drosophila central neuron, or in cultured Drosophila neurons, within the time budget. That is the single largest gap in this topic.
- Searched but did not find a Drosophila NMDA paper matching "Ihara 2005" — likely a misattribution or a non-Drosophila (possibly honeybee or vertebrate pharmacology) reference; flagging rather than guessing.
- Ultsch, Schuster, Werner, Laube, Pirooz Sheikh, Betz, Schmitt, Walter? — the cloning/primary-structure paper is **Ultsch et al. 1993, FEBS Letters 324:171-177** ("Glutamate receptors of Drosophila melanogaster: Primary structure of a putative NMDA receptor protein expressed in the head of the adult fly"), not PNAS as I initially guessed — SECONDARY read (search synthesis only), a cloning/sequence paper, not an electrophysiology paper with Mg2+ block constants.

**Mg2+ in external saline / hemolymph:**
- **Wilson lab adult-fly extracellular saline (used for in vivo whole-cell PN/LN recording)**: **4 mM MgCl2**. MEASURED/reported, full text, read independently in two papers' Methods sections: Wilson & Laurent 2005 J Neurosci (Materials and Methods, saline recipe above) and Turner, Bazhenov & Laurent 2008 J Neurophysiol 99:734-746 (Methods: "Extracellular saline contained (in mM) 103 NaCl, 3 KCl, **4 MgCl2**..., 1 NaH2PO4, 10 trehalose, and 5 glucose"). Two independent full-text reads agree on 4 mM — good internal consistency for "the saline used in this lab's in vivo adult recordings," though it is a prepared recording saline, not hemolymph itself.
- **Standard "Adult Hemolymph-Like Saline" (AHLS)** recipe (Cold Spring Harbor Protocols, 2013, pdb.rec079459): reported as CaCl2 2 mM, KCl 5 mM, HEPES 5 mM, **MgCl2 8.2 mM**, NaCl 108 mM, NaHCO3 4 mM, NaH2PO4 1 mM, sucrose 10 mM, trehalose 5 mM, pH 7.5, 265 mOsm. SECONDARY — the CSHL Protocols page itself returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; this composition is reconstructed from WebSearch result snippets of that page, not independently verified against the primary page text. Treat the exact MgCl2 figure (8.2 mM) as needing a primary-source check before being called "measured."
- **Hemolymph Mg2+ (whole, not free-ionized)**: reported via WebSearch synthesis as **~26 mM in adult Drosophila and ~33 mM in larvae** (Larrivee 1979, atomic absorption spectrophotometry) — SECONDARY, I could not reach Larrivee 1979 directly in the time budget, this number is relayed from a WebSearch summary, not read from the primary paper or even a secondary paper's verbatim quote. Important caveat (my own inference, not from any source): atomic absorption spectrophotometry measures *total* Mg2+ (free + protein/ATP-bound), which is known in other insects/vertebrates to considerably overestimate free ionized Mg2+ relevant to NMDAR block — this likely explains why hemolymph-total-Mg (~26 mM) looks much higher than what electrophysiologists put in their recording salines (4-8 mM). This reconciliation is my own reasoning, flagged as such, not a sourced claim.

**Bottom line for Topic 3**: solid, full-text-sourced numbers exist for (a) the Mg2+ content of the standard in vivo recording saline used by the Wilson/Laurent lab (4 mM, two independent full-text reads) and (b) qualitative, full-text-sourced evidence that Drosophila NMDARs (dNR1/dNR2) show voltage-dependent Mg2+ block in a heterologous system, with an atypical pore residue (Q721) relative to vertebrate GluN2. There is no recorded central-neuron NMDA synaptic current, no amplitude ratio, no decay tau, and no quantitative IC50/block constant found anywhere in this search. The hemolymph total-Mg number (26 mM) and the AHLS recipe (8.2 mM) are both secondary-level and should be re-verified before being labeled "measured" in the model's registry.

## 4. In vivo membrane-potential noise

**Kenyon cells — Turner, Bazhenov & Laurent 2008, J Neurophysiol 99:734-746**, "Olfactory Representations by Drosophila Mushroom Body Neurons." Read as full text (PDF converted to text from the open Salk/Bazhenov-lab mirror). Conditions: in vivo whole-cell current clamp, **Canton-S, all female** flies (Methods: "All flies were wild-type Canton-S females"); saline 103 NaCl, 3 KCl, **4 MgCl2**, TES, 1 NaH2PO4, 10 trehalose, 5 glucose (matches Wilson & Laurent 2005's Mg2+); recording temperature not stated (gap).
- KC holding potential: **-58 ± 2 (SD) mV** in current clamp (this is cell-to-cell variability in resting Vm across the population, not within-cell temporal noise). MEASURED, full text, Results ("in vivo" section).
- Membrane time constant of KCs, measured at the soma by hyperpolarizing current injection: **>200 ms** ("very long"). MEASURED, full text, Results (Synaptic drive from PNs to KCs / QUANTAL EVENTS section). No more precise number given — reported only as a lower bound.
- Spontaneous/miniature EPSP statistics (quantal unitary events, not aggregate Vm noise, but the building block of it): mean EPSP amplitude **1.4 ± 0.8 mV** (control), falling to **1.0 ± 0.5 mV** after TTX (1 µM) with a drop in the distribution's upper tail — implying most spontaneous EPSPs are spike-driven synaptic release rather than pure miniatures. EPSP 10-90% rise time **2.1 ± 0.5 ms**; EPSP decay **11.5 ± 5.3 ms** (single exponential fit). From underlying EPSCs (voltage clamp, Vhold = -60 mV, n=5 flies): rise time **0.9 ± 0.4 ms**, decay tau **2.8 ± 1.2 ms**. Spontaneous EPSP frequency **32.6 ± 12.7 s⁻¹ (n=27 KCs)**; spontaneous PN firing rate in the same conditions **4.6 ± 4.2 s⁻¹ (n=37 PNs)**. All MEASURED, full text, Fig. 3 and associated Results text.
- **No single number for "SD of subthreshold Vm at rest" (as an aggregate baseline noise statistic, e.g. from a quiet baseline epoch) is reported in this paper** — the paper characterizes noise via discrete EPSP events (amplitude/frequency/kinetics), not via an overall Vm variance. If the model needs a baseline Vm-noise SD for KCs, it is not directly measured here and would have to be *derived* from the EPSP statistics above plus a KC membrane model (Poisson synaptic bombardment with these EPSP amplitudes/rates filtered by a >200 ms membrane tau) — I have not done that derivation; flagging it as a legitimate derivation path rather than doing it here, since the user asked me not to invent numbers.
- Also reported (MEASURED, full text, Results): difference between resting potential and spike threshold, 17 KCs, **21.5 ± 5.6 mV**; average KC holding potential in that same sub-analysis, **-57.8 mV**.

**Lobula-plate tangential cells (VS cells) — Maimon, Straw & Dickinson 2010, Nature Neuroscience 13:393-399**, "Active flight increases the gain of visual motion processing in Drosophila." Read as full text (PDF converted to text from the open Maimon-lab mirror). Conditions: in vivo whole-cell current/voltage clamp, tethered **flying** flies; **2-3 day old females** (Methods: "We studied 2-3-d-old female Drosophila melanogaster"); dissection bath initially 30°C then **lowered to 20°C for the remainder of the experiment** (explicit recording temperature, unlike the olfactory papers above); mean resting potential without current injection **-53 mV**; for other cells, soma hyperpolarized by injected current to **-55 to -60 mV**.
- Figure 4d-e: sample baseline Vm traces and a bar graph of **"Standard deviation of Vm (mV)"** for pre-flight / flight / post-flight epochs (n up to 45 cells, at least one qualifying 1-s-baseline trial from 40/45 cells). The text reports only that the rise in variance of the baseline Vm trace during flight vs. non-flight is statistically significant (**t test, P = 8.4 × 10⁻¹²**); the y-axis of Fig. 4e is scaled 0-2 mV, implying typical SD values are on the order of a few tenths of a mV to ~1-2 mV, but I could not extract the exact bar heights/error bars from the text-converted PDF — **this specific SD value is read-from-figure-axis-range only, not a quoted number**, so treat it as approximate/figure-level, not a precise measured value until someone reads the actual bar heights off the figure image. Mark LOW CONFIDENCE.
- A tonic depolarization of baseline Vm during flight vs. non-flight of **1-2 mV** is explicitly stated in text (not just read off a figure). MEASURED, full text.
- Passive membrane resistance (Rm) example cell: **209 MΩ pre-flight, 187 MΩ during flight, 209 MΩ post-flight** (one example cell, Fig. 4 legend) — population data show a statistically significant drop in Rm during flight (t-test, exact P not captured in my extraction). MEASURED, full text, Fig. 4c legend.
- Voltage-clamp current-noise analog: SD of holding current at -93 mV also rises significantly during flight (**t test, P = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵**), consistent with increased synaptic/channel noise, not just Vm noise per se. MEASURED, full text, Fig. 4f-g.
- No membrane time constant explicitly stated in the portion I could extract (may be present in Online Methods not captured by my text conversion) — gap.

## Summary (5 lines)

(a) Values usable as "inside a measured range, source cited": the 46±5%/~54% GABA-A/GABA-B split of focal-GABA-puff IPSP amplitude in antennal lobe PNs (Wilson & Laurent 2005, Fig. 2B/C, full text); the 4 mM MgCl2 in the standard Wilson-lab adult in vivo saline (two independent full-text confirmations); KC EPSP/EPSC amplitude, rise and decay kinetics and KC membrane tau (>200 ms) (Turner et al. 2008, Fig. 3, full text); the tonic 1-2 mV flight-induced Vm depolarization and the Rm drop during flight in VS cells (Maimon et al. 2010, Fig. 4, full text).
(b) Candidates for an independent held-out check, but not yet corroborated or not yet quantified: no second postsynaptic GABA-B amplitude-ratio/tau measurement in a non-olfactory cell type was found (real gap, worth another dedicated search pass); the VS-cell "SD of Vm" number is figure-only (approximate, needs the actual figure read, not just axis range); mAChR-A/B and DmGluRA have zero recorded slow-synaptic-current numbers anywhere found (both are calcium-imaging-only evidence so far) and should not be assigned a biophysical tau/amplitude by analogy; NMDA receptor Mg2+ block in Drosophila is characterized only in heterologous S2-cell/oocyte systems with no IC50 and no central-neuron recording, and the hemolymph total-Mg figure (26 mM, Larrivee 1979) is secondary and needs primary-source verification before being labeled measured.

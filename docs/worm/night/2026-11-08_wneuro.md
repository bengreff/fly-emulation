## Item 1, the defecation chain (Wang et al. 2013 PMC3651789: one DVB spike per cycle, 51 of 51)

**1c, class A, fixed.** Measured geometry (`scripts/worm/wneuro/geom.py`, BAAIWorm 3D segments, the pool's four
compartments cut at soma y -311.65 / -200 / 61 / 330 / 410.15 um): AVL's soma is in the head but 79% of its membrane
area lies behind it; DVB's soma is in the tail but 71% of its membrane is in the posterior body, where the intestine
releases NLP-40. Reading at the soma left AVL at 0.86 nM (max) while the posterior body held tens of nM. Release
stays at the soma's compartment: HSNL has 65% of its membrane in the anterior body but all 23 of its BAAIWorm
presynaptic sites in the head (measured), so splitting release by area would be wrong. First membrane run (release
split by area, superseded): AVL read 104 nM, occupancy 0.63, DVB 214 nM (measured). Final run (release at the soma):
AVL read 104.4 nM (max; median 67.8), occupancy 0.63, Gs 0.63; DVB read 213.8 nM (max; median 139.3), occupancy 0.94, Gs 0.94; 0 spikes in AVL or DVB in any cycle, v max -33.8 and -32.2 mV (measured). The NLP-40 here is the intestine's, released straight into the posterior body, so moving neuronal release back to the soma left these numbers unchanged.

**1b, class B, UNC-2 ported, the rest blocked.** Wang and Sieburth 2013 put UNC-2 and EGL-19 downstream of PKA and
say "other non voltage-gated calcium channels must be also required"; that third target has no published identity or
kinetics, so it is not built. Two measurements say the line cannot close inside neuromod:
- Gs clamped at 1 in DVB for 38 s gives no spike (median -50.8 mV, measured).
- DVB's membrane cannot make an action potential: 20 pA injected plateaus near -19 mV (v max -9.1 mV), against Jiang
  et al. 2022's (PMC9120479) peak +26.3 mV from rest -49.0, threshold -23.4 mV (measured both). That is the cell
  model's (wbrain2's); request below.

**1a, D at the range edge; the cause is upstream.**
- In brain (`dmp_trace.py` + `probe_a.py`, bar written 22:51, amended 22:59, before running), DVB's AEX-2 occupancy
  modulation per cycle (cycles 2, 3): tau 100 s (template) about 0; tau 30 s about 0; tau 10 s 0.025 / -0.001; corner
  release (R 100 nM/s, k 1.0 uM, n 4) with tau 10 s 0.187 / -0.001, with tau 30 s 0 / 0 (all measured). All fail.
- Two causes in the intestine's release law (organs_intestine, wbody2's): (i) a tonic part, Hill(0.09 uM; k 0.3,
  n 2) = 8% of the maximum at basal Ca (derived); (ii) release follows the mean Hill(Ca) of all 20 intestinal cells,
  which is not locked to the int9 peak (cycle 1 the mean peaks about 15 s after int9, cycle 3 about 14 s before it,
  tau10k run, measured). Wang 2013's spike is locked to the posterior contraction.

## Item 5, homeostasis set point

`scripts/worm/wneuro/homeo_setpoint.py` (bar written 23:16, before running). The template brain is near its basal
Ca in the set window and after it: c0 median 0.0506 uM, later mean 0.0503 uM (measured); the start-up transient is
under 1% (median 0-0.5 s vs 1.5-2 s -0.8%, measured). So the 2 s window does not catch a transient, and the rule
is not class A. The one-sidedness is real but comes from two things that are not code defects:
(i) the published rule's own bound, err = (c0 - Ca) / c0 <= 1 because Ca >= 0 (O'Leary et al. 2014), and
(ii) the open-loop brain's resting state: most neurons sit at the basal Ca floor during the set window, so later
activity can only push err negative, and densities can only fall. The cited C. elegans measurement is in that
direction (PMC11363341: UNC-2 falls after more than 2 h of stimulation); up-regulation after silencing is not
cited in the module and not looked for tonight. Re-check after wbrain2's bulk Ca pool merges (it changes the Ca the
rule reads).

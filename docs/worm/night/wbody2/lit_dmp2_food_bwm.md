## 1. AVL/DVB during the DMP — Jiang et al. 2022, *Nat Commun* 13:2783, "C. elegans enteric motor neurons fire synchronized action potentials underlying the defecation motor program"

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9120479/ — **full text opened** (PMC).

- Resting potential: DVB **-49.0 ± 3.6 mV (n=5)**; AVL **-45.7 ± 1.1 mV (n=17)**.
- AP threshold: DVB **-23.4 ± 1.6 mV**; AVL **-23.3 ± 0.8 mV**.
- AP peak amplitude: DVB **26.3 ± 2.6 mV**; AVL **9.8 ± 0.8 mV** (AVL's AP is unusually small/biphasic —
  described elsewhere in the paper as having a fast positive spike immediately followed by a negative spike).
- Afterhyperpolarization (AHP) trough: DVB **-38.5 ± 2.5 mV**; AVL **-64.8 ± 1.2 mV**. AHP duration **~40 s**,
  "matching the duration of the intercycle."
- Spike half-width: DVB **358.8 ± 59.5 ms**; AVL **394.2 ± 40.0 ms**.
- **One AP (compound spike) per DMP cycle** in each of AVL and DVB (not a burst) — consistent with "synchronized
  calcium/voltage spikes" language throughout; the paper does not describe multiple APs per cycle in WT.
- Channels: "UNC-2 plays a primary role in mediating the positive action potential in AVL, possibly with minor
  contributions from EGL-19" (CaV2 P/Q-type and CaV1 L-type respectively). The negative/repolarizing phase of the
  AVL spike is generated by **EXP-2**, described as a repolarization-activated K+ channel. NCA-1/NCA-2 double
  mutant: "AVL action potentials recorded in these mutants were largely unchanged compared to those in the
  wild type" — i.e. NCA channels are not required for the AVL AP. EGL-36 (Kv3) appears only in the paper's
  biophysical model, not as an experimentally tested channel in this paper.
- Timing: "Synchronized calcium spikes in AVL and DVB coincided with anterior body muscle contractions and
  expulsions but lagged about 3 s behind the intestinal calcium wave." AVL spikes precede DVB spikes by
  **215 ± 176 ms**. Expulsion was "reliably detected in 94.7% cycles (18 out of 19 cycles) and was always
  associated with synchronized AVL/DVB spikes."
- Trigger: "the intestinal rhythmicity is relayed to the enteric neurons via calcium-mediated release of a
  neuropeptide-like protein NLP-40 from the intestine, activating the AEX-2/G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR)
  on AVL and DVB." Direct test: "injection of neuropeptide NLP-40 into the tail region evoked DVB calcium
  transients that are indistinguishable from spontaneously occurring ones." No endogenous NLP-40 concentration
  or timing-precision number is given beyond this.
- Current-clamp numbers used in the paper's own protocols (not necessarily "threshold to fire" but the
  stimulus protocols used): AVL stimulus "3-s long at 2 pA" (Fig 6f, used for a different manipulation);
  step protocol "5 s long in 1 pA increments" for both AVL and DVB (Fig 1a/b, used to characterize passive/
  active properties); a modeling section states "stimulating current was set to 10 pA to reliably evoke an
  action potential" (this is from the paper's companion biophysical model, not a direct current-clamp
  measurement on biological cells — flagged as such); AHP protocol "1-s-long 8 pA stimulus in WT" (Fig 6g).
- Enteric muscle contraction: the paper states "coordinated contraction and relaxation of enteric muscle
  groups finally lead to discrete expulsion events" but **does not give a duration number for the muscle
  contraction itself**. In *exp-2(lf)* mutants, "in ~30% of total cycles, an additional expulsion was observed
  immediately following the normally timed expulsion," with inter-expulsion delay "430 ± 155 ms" — this is a
  mutant phenotype number, not WT.

Not checked in this pass: the bioRxiv preprint of this same paper (2021.08.25.457687) exists and might have
slightly different numbers/supplementary detail; not opened (time budget).

## 2. Wang et al. 2013, *Curr Biol* 23:746-54, "Neuropeptide Secreted from a Pacemaker Activates Neurons to Control a Rhythmic Behavior" (NLP-40/AEX-2)

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3651789/ — **full text opened** (PMC). The cell.com version
(https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(13)00351-5) returned **403 Forbidden**, not opened.

- DVB Ca2+ transient: "a single robust calcium spike in the synaptic region of the DVB neuron... began
  approximately three seconds following each pBoc step, peaked immediately before each Exp step" —
  **51 of 51 cycles** in WT showed this; **0 of 46 cycles (14 worms)** in nlp-40 mutants showed a spike.
  This directly answers Q2: yes, one Ca transient in DVB per cycle, NLP-40-dependent.
- Injected NLP-40 peptide reproduces the transient: "the onset delay and duration of the induced calcium
  spikes in DVB were similar to those induced by endogenously released NLP-40" (quantitative delay/duration
  values were not extracted in this pass — would need a closer read of the figure legend).
- AEX-2/GPCR: "activation of AEX-2/GPCR triggers calcium influx in AVL and DVB neurons." **The paper does
  not show, by current clamp or otherwise, that AEX-2 activation directly depolarizes the neurons** — the
  demonstrated effect is calcium influx/transients, not membrane voltage. This is an important gap for the
  model: AEX-2 is shown to raise Ca2+, not shown here to be a depolarizing conductance per se.
- Gαs/PKA: the paper proposes a pathway "Gαs adenylate cyclase (ACY) to produce cAMP" but explicitly states
  (confirmed on a second, targeted re-read) **"the downstream effectors of cAMP are unknown"** — i.e. **PKA is
  not tested in this paper**. A ResearchGate figure caption found via search ("PKA is necessary for calcium
  influx in DVB neurons") does **not** appear in this paper's full text when searched directly; it most likely
  belongs to a different (later) paper, and I could not confirm its source — **do not use that PKA claim**,
  it is unverified and may be misattributed.
- EC50: "P3-1 EC50 = 3.39 nM ± 1.17 nM"; "P3-2 EC50 = 26.73 nM ± 1.18 nM"; "P1 peptide failed to activate
  AEX-2/GPCR" (these are NLP-40-derived peptide fragments tested on heterologously expressed AEX-2, per the
  paper's cell-based assay — conditions/cell system not re-confirmed in this pass, flag for closer read if
  the EC50 number is load-bearing).

## 3. Enteric muscles during expulsion; EXP-1 (Beg & Jorgensen 2003)

- Three muscle groups cooperate at expulsion: "the intestinal muscle, sphincter muscle, and anal depressor
  muscle... contract and relax in synchrony to execute a successful expulsion" (from WebSearch synthesis of
  multiple sources including the G3 2017 male-circuitry paper and Jiang 2022 bioRxiv preprint — **not a single
  directly-opened full-text quote**, treat as a paraphrase pending direct confirmation).
- Jiang 2022 full text (opened, see above) does not give a contraction-duration number for the enteric
  muscles themselves; it only establishes that contraction/relaxation of these groups produces "discrete
  expulsion events," i.e. **one contraction per cycle in WT** (the only multi-contraction phenotype reported

  - No PMC entry found for this paper (searched; pre-dates the NIH public access mandate and Nat Neurosci

  - The only numbers available (single-channel conductance **29 pS main conductance, 20 pS and 9 pS

## 4. DMP period on vs off food — Thomas 1990 *Genetics* 124:855; Liu & Thomas 1994 *J Neurosci* 14:1953

- Thomas 1990: https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/124/4/855/5999973 and
  https://www.genetics.org/content/124/4/855 — **both returned abstract/metadata only**; the Oxford Academic
  page explicitly told the fetch tool "the actual data tables and detailed results are not included... would
  need a subscription." **Full text not obtained.**
- Liu & Thomas 1994: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6577142/ — fetched; the PMC record's extractable
  text was abstract-level only (the full-text pages are pre-OCR scanned images that the fetch tool could not
  read as text). Abstract-level content obtained: "a three-part motor program mediates a defecation every
  45 sec in well-fed wild-type C. elegans"; "Individual worms maintain this 45 sec rhythm with an SD of about
  3 sec"; "animals away from food rarely activated the motor program"; "food dilution resulted in a graded
  lengthening of the cycle period." **No off-food numeric period (minutes/hours) was found** — the statement
  is qualitative ("rarely activated"), not a measured off-food period in seconds. The jneurosci.org PDF
  (https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/14/4/1953.full.pdf) returned **403 Forbidden**, not opened.
- Secondary-source paraphrase (NCBI Bookshelf "C. elegans II" chapter on defecation, found via WebSearch
  snippet; **the Bookshelf page itself returned a reCAPTCHA wall on direct fetch, so this was not verified
  by reading the page**): "When wild type worms spontaneously leave a lawn of food, the contractions of the
  motor program cease, and on return to food the next defecation tends to be in phase with the original
  cycle... When an animal is gently touched at various times during a defecation cycle, the next DMP occurs
  45 seconds after the touch, suggesting that the clock has been reset to zero." **Flag: this is a
  WebSearch-tool paraphrase of an unopened page, not a direct quote — treat as low confidence, especially the
  specific "cease" claim and the touch-reset claim.** It does NOT support a specific off-food period of
  "45-50 s continuing" — if anything it suggests the overt motor program stops (no visible DMP) while an
  internal clock/phase may persist. This directly contradicts part of the question's framing ("does the cycle
  stay at about 45-50 s off food") — based on what I could verify, the honest answer is: **I could not
  obtain a verified numeric off-food period; what little text I got (secondhand) says contractions cease
  off food, not that they continue at 45-50 s.**

## 6. Food → pumping via serotonin

- Rhoades et al. 2019 *Cell* 176:85-97 ("ASICs Mediate Food Responses in an Enteric Serotonergic Neuron...").
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6526957/ — full text opened. "NSM acutely responds to the
  ingestion of bacterial food in a manner that does not require synaptic or neuropeptidergic inputs," via a
  "heat-insensitive component of bacterial membranes." "NSM calcium events were abolished in fasted del-3;
  del-7 double mutants and greatly attenuated in starved double mutants" — i.e. DEL-3/DEL-7 (ASIC channels)
  are required for food-triggered NSM Ca2+ activity. Note: "del-3;del-7 mutants had normal food ingestion
  rates" (Fig S3A) — i.e. loss of these channels does NOT reduce pumping rate itself; their effect is on
  NSM-driven locomotor slowing, not on pumping. **No direct Hz/pumps-per-minute number for del-3;del-7 vs WT
  pumping was found in this pass.**
- Serotonin/tph-1 and pumping rate: Source
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7255878/ (microfluidic electropharyngeogram paper) — full text
  opened. "C. elegans tph-1 mutants exhibited significantly lower pharyngeal pumping rates than N2 control
  animals (N2 = 3.46 ± 0.19 Hz; tph-1 = 0.89 ± 0.10 Hz; mean ± SEM)." Conditions: N2 wild-type (n=90) vs
  MT15434 tph-1(mg280) (n=92); on food (100 mg/ml E. coli OP50 in M9 buffer); 2-min recording after a 2-h
  fast, during the elevated feeding phase; p<0.0001 (two-tailed t-test); temperature not specified in the
  extracted text. This is a ~4-fold reduction in pumping with tph-1 loss, directly answering Q6's numeric ask.
- Other routes from food to pumping rate (one line each, not independently verified in full text this pass,
  from search snippets only — flag as low confidence / not opened):
  - Pharyngeal neurons MC (cholinergic) and M3/M4 drive the fast pump cycle itself; 5-HT is reported elsewhere
    (Niacaris & Avery 2003, not opened here) to act partly via MC.
  - I1/I2 pharyngeal interneurons are implicated in food-dependent pumping modulation in some papers (not
    opened here, name-only from background knowledge of the field — flagged, not sourced in this session).
  - Glutamatergic input (from ADF or similar) has been proposed in some papers as a parallel route; **not
    checked or sourced in this session — do not treat as confirmed.**

## 7. Body-wall muscle action potentials

- Gao & Zhen 2011, *PNAS* 108:2557, "Action potentials drive body wall muscle contractions in C. elegans."
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3038695/ — full text opened, but **dissection method
  detail (glued/cut/cuticle opened) was not retrievable** from the fetch (relegated to supplementary methods
  not rendered as text by the tool). Numbers obtained:
  - Pipette (intracellular) solution: 115 mM K-gluconate, 25 mM KCl, 0.1 mM CaCl2, 5 mM MgCl2, 1 mM BAPTA,
    10 mM Hepes, 5 mM Na2ATP, 0.5 mM Na2GTP, 0.5 mM cAMP, 0.5 mM cGMP.
  - Extracellular/bath solution: 150 mM NaCl, 5 mM KCl, 5 mM CaCl2, 1 mM MgCl2, 10 mM glucose, 5 mM sucrose,
    15 mM Hepes.
  - Recording temperature: **not found in the extracted text** (likely room temperature per convention for
    this prep, but not stated — do not assume a number).
  - Resting potential: **-25.0 ± 1.0 mV (n=27)**, noted in-paper as "considerably more depolarized than...
    vertebrate skeletal muscle."
  - AP peak amplitude and half-width: **52.6 ± 1.8 mV and 15.5 ± 0.9 ms**.
  - Spontaneous firing frequency: **0.73 ± 0.14 Hz**.
  - Channels: "L-VGCC/EGL-19 is the main Ca2+ channel eliciting action potentials"; "Kv1/SHK-1 affects the
    action potential termination."
- Liu P, Ge Q, Chen B, Salkoff L, Kotlikoff MI, Wang Z-W 2011, *J Physiol* 589:101-117, "Genetic dissection
  of ion currents underlying all-or-none action potentials in C. elegans body-wall muscle cells." **Full text
  NOT opened** — Wiley page returned 403 Forbidden, no free PMC mirror found (J Physiol is not systematically
  open for 2011 content and this paper did not turn up in PMC). Numbers reported here (EGL-19 L-type Ca2+
  channel and SHK-1/SLO-2 K+ channels as key contributors) come only from WebSearch snippets of the abstract
  — **no resting potential or firing-rate number from this specific paper was obtained**; don't conflate with
  the Gao & Zhen numbers above, which are a different paper/prep.
- Intact (non-dissected) worm BWM spike rate or resting potential: **none found**. A 2024/2025 biophysical-
  modeling paper (PMC11781704, "Biophysical modeling and experimental analysis of the dynamics of C. elegans
  body-wall muscle cells") was checked specifically for this and does not provide an intact-worm number; it
  only restates "calcium-dependent action potentials at frequencies between 3-10 Hz" as its own summary of
  cited dissected-prep literature, not a new intact-animal measurement. **No free source with an intact-worm
  BWM voltage or spike-rate estimate was located in this session.**

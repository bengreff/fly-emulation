- Row 14 (21:00, before any run of the intestine). Model: 20 intestinal cells (int1 x 4, int2-int9 x 2), each a
  Li & Rinzel 1994 IP3R Ca cell (De Young-Keizer rates as in Ermentrout's LiRinzel.ode), IP3 made by PLC (a basal
  PLC-gamma rate plus a Ca-stimulated term, Teramoto & Iwasaki 2006's Ca -> PLC loop; rates guessed) and degraded;
  INX-16 gaps pass Ca and IP3 between neighbouring cells (permeability guessed, not tuned to the wave); int9 is the
  pacemaker through a higher basal PLC (inferred from where the wave starts). One value is fitted: the IP3R
  recovery rate a2, set so the isolated int9 cell's free period is 49 s (Espelt 2005). Check on the isolated
  intestine (numpy, dt 0.5 ms, 600 s, no neurons). Scored: (1) the coupled intestine's period 45-52 s (consistency
  only, since a2 is fitted); (2) the wave starts in int9 and the ring peak times run posterior to anterior in at
  least 7 of the 8 ring steps; (3) wave velocity int9 -> int2 between 20 and 60 um/s (Espelt 2005: 33 +- 3 um/s;
  itr-1 gain of function 64-91), 80 um per ring (guessed); (4) falsifier: IP3R flux 0 (itr-1 null) leaves no Ca
  transient above 10 % of the wild-type amplitude in 600 s. Reported, not scored: rise and fall time (Espelt 8 +- 1
  s and 23 +- 3 s) and the posterior cleft pH minimum and its lag after the int9 Ca peak (Beg 2008: acidification
  rise 1.9 s, decay 2.9 s, about 2 s before pBoc).

  bwm_gap_liu_nS uses 0.05-0.35 nS. The old bwm_gap row (5 nS) stays with the off bwm_coupling toggle.

  mech_out['bwm_ca_mM']).

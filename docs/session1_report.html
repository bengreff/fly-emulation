Fly emulation · Session 1 · 14 September 2026

What the connectome doesn't contain

The published rhythm reproduces cleanly, and the anatomy checks out against independent electrophysiology. Then the night turned into an audit. The wiring diagram tells you which neurons talk to which. It does not tell you whether a connection excites or inhibits, how strong it is, or how excitable a cell is, and in this model all three are supplied by convention. Several of those conventions turn out to be load-bearing, and at least one is simply wrong.

What this was

You asked for grounding. So instead of building infrastructure I took the strongest published connectome-constrained simulation of the fly's motor system, reproduced it independently, and then attacked it with the controls this project's own documents demand. What I did not expect was how much of the night would be spent finding that my own first answers were wrong.

The subject is the ventral nerve cord, the fly's spinal-cord equivalent, which drives the legs. A recent study built a 4,604-neuron model of its front-leg circuitry directly from the wiring diagram, stimulated one descending command neuron, and reported rhythmic motor output resembling a stepping pattern generator. That is the right thing to stand on, and the first thing to check.

Terms used below

Connectome
A complete electron-microscope wiring diagram: every neuron and every synapse, in one individual animal.
Ventral nerve cord
The fly's spinal-cord equivalent. Contains the circuits driving legs and wings.
Descending neuron
Carries commands from brain to nerve cord. DNg100 and DNa02 are two named ones.
Motor neuron
The output cell. Its spikes contract one muscle. The front leg has 144.
Proprioceptor
A sensor reporting the limb's own position, movement or load.
Rhythmicity
A 0–1 score for how periodic activity is. 0.8 is a clean oscillation; 0.1 is noise.

The headline

Two things matter about this model's output: how rhythmic it is, and how hard the motor neurons actually fire. A leg needs both. Over the night I varied synaptic strength, sensory drive, which command neuron is stimulated, where current is injected, how synaptic sign is assigned, and how excitability is computed. Then I pooled every simulation and plotted both readouts together.

The boundary is sharp, and it does not depend on where I draw the thresholds. Asking, for each floor on one axis, the best value any simulation reached on the other:

The wall sits between 8.5 and 10 Hz on one axis and between 0.31 and 0.4 on the other. The only thing that ever crossed it was the scrambled connectome.

This is not a defect in someone's code. It is a property of the model class: a nerve cord cut off from its body, driven by a constant injected current, with one global number converting synapse counts into strength, sign guessed from a transmitter prediction, and excitability derived from cell size. Knowing precisely where that class breaks, and which of those conventions is responsible, is the useful thing. The rest of this report is that.

Part one — what holds up

The result reproduces, and the anatomy is sound

F1

A units bug in the published analysis code

The oscillation-frequency function in the reference repository builds its time axis from sample indices, so it returns cycles per sample, not hertz. At the default 1 ms step the value needs multiplying by 1000. Caught because a visibly ten-per-second rhythm was reported as 0.01 Hz. A periodogram estimate now runs alongside every frequency the pipeline reports, and a test pins a synthetic 20 Hz signal.

F2

The anatomy joins cleanly, and outside measurement agrees

All 144 motor neurons in the simulated network matched a nerve-cord annotation on exact integer identifier, each with a named target muscle and traced status. No silent losses.

The corroboration is the valuable part. The tibia flexor pool, which bends the lower leg, contains exactly 15 motor neurons per side in the wiring diagram. An electrophysiology study that never touched this connectome reports tibia flexion controlled by "approximately 15 motor neurons". Two independent methods agree, and cell body size across the pool spans 13-fold, matching the size gradient that study describes.

F3

The rhythm reproduces. The amplitude does not.

A strong rhythm near 10 Hz appeared in every draw. But the model's peak is thirteen times below the resting rate of the one leg motor neuron whose rate has been measured. The units that make usable force, roughly one fly body weight per spike, never approach threshold. Only three motor neurons fire at all, all serving hip muscles on one side. The entire lower leg stays silent.

F4

The specific wiring is doing real work

The control the project documents demand: compare against a carefully matched alternative. The shuffle preserves every neuron's outgoing connection count and weights exactly, and the excitatory–inhibitory composition of each class. Only which cell receives each connection changes.

Shuffling makes the network both hyperactive and arrhythmic: twenty times more cells active, rhythm largely gone. The pattern of connections, not merely how many each cell has, holds this circuit in a sparse oscillating regime. This is the strongest evidence of the night that the anatomy carries real functional information.

Part two — what blocks a walking leg

The pieces do not meet

A data gap found by trying to fix it. The definitive study of the fly's main leg proprioceptor uses calcium imaging. It reports which subgroup encodes position, direction and vibration, and over what joint-angle range. It reports no firing rates. There is no published rate calibration with which to drive these neurons. Every sensory amplitude used tonight is a swept unknown, recorded as a scaffold in the run record, never as a measurement.
F6

The best available fly body can receive two thirds of the motor output

The body-platform question is largely closed: one package now ships the walking body, the flight body, a musculoskeletal model and GPU physics. But the muscle model exposes 15 actuators on the left front leg only, with the other leg locked.

Absent entirely: the femur reductor, all tarsus muscles, meaning no active foot, and the long tendon muscles. Worse, the 15-neuron tibia flexor pool, the one group with excellent physiology, collapses onto a single actuator, so the size principle the fly uses to grade force cannot be expressed. Extending the muscle model is prerequisite work, not a detail.

Notice the last row. With no stimulus the network is exactly silent, all 4,604 cells at zero. Real nervous systems never are. Whatever supplies that baseline in the animal is absent here, and it is plausibly part of what sets resting motor-neuron rates in the first place.

An interlude: how I got this wrong, twice

The most useful thing that happened tonight was a finding that survived neither of its own follow-ups. It is worth showing because it is exactly the failure mode this project needs to be protected against.

First answerSwitching the silent proprioceptors on destroys the rhythm. So does driving random interneurons. Conclusion: it is total excitation, not the sensory pathway. Evidence: one replicate, one amplitude.
ContradictedReplicated at eight draws and a lower amplitude, driving random interneurons does nothing (0.830 against a 0.836 baseline) while the same drive into proprioceptors collapses the rhythm to 0.095. Conclusion reversed: the sensory pathway is privileged.
Explained awayThe model scales spike threshold with cell size. Proprioceptors are 0.44× median size, bristles 0.078×. The same current is a far bigger push. Matching drive to each cell's own threshold, the difference disappears.

The first answer was right by accident, the second was wrong for a reason that looked like biology and was arithmetic, and only the third is a control. All three are kept in the findings file with the retraction visible, rather than quietly edited.

F8b

Apparent pathway specificity was an excitability artefact

F9

Forty-one percent of the inhibition rests on a convention

Glutamatergic cells are 23.8% of the network and supply 40.8% of its inhibition. Excitation and inhibition onto motor neurons are nearly balanced, a ratio of 1.02. And in Drosophila, glutamate is inhibitory where a glutamate-gated chloride channel is expressed postsynaptically and excitatory where it is not. The sign is a property of the receptor, not the transmitter, and the connectome does not contain receptor identity.

F10

The model gives the fly's main leg proprioceptor the wrong sign

Following the drive-target control led to something specific and checkable. The transmitter classifier is markedly less sure about sensory neurons than about anything else, and on those uncertain calls it labels most leg afferents glutamatergic, which this model renders inhibitory.

The primary literature describes the femoral chordotonal organ, the fly's principal leg proprioceptor, as roughly 150 excitatory cholinergic neurons, in claw, hook and club subtypes encoding position, movement direction and vibration. Insect mechanosensory afferents are cholinergic as a class.

So the model assigns an inhibitory sign to the majority of the neurons that should be the excitatory sensory drive into the leg motor circuit, on classifier calls whose own confidence is below 0.7. An override now forces them excitatory on the published evidence. It does not by itself restore the rhythm, so this is not the explanation for F7. It is a separate defect, and it is the one a reader can act on tomorrow.

F11

Excitability comes from a volume that means different things for different cells

The model scales each neuron's gain and threshold by its soma size, normalised to the network median. That is a defensible heuristic. But size is a segmentation volume measured inside the nerve cord, and not every class has its cell body there.

Relative size, resulting excitability, and where the cell body actually sits
ClassnRel. sizeGainSoma location

By subclass it is starker: mechanosensory bristles land at 0.078 of the median, a 12.9× gain multiplier. The two populations carrying input into this circuit, sensory afferents and descending neurons, are exactly the two whose measured volume is an arbor rather than a cell. They are handed an order-of-magnitude excitability advantage that is an artefact of where the microscope volume was cropped.

This is not a calibration detail. It means a flat injected current is a wildly different perturbation depending on which class receives it, which makes any perturbation experiment in this model hard to read. F8b is a worked example of the trap. The fix is to normalise within class, use a quantity that means the same thing everywhere, or drop size scaling and fit excitability per cell type against physiology.

F12

Rhythmic sensory drive does not rescue it, at any frequency

This was designed to be the discriminator, and it came back negative, which is the useful kind. If the rhythm dies under sensory drive because constant input is an unrealistic caricature of what a moving leg supplies, then giving the same mean drive a temporal structure should help.

The motor output does not even entrain to the drive: it sits near 1.5 to 2.4 Hz whether the input is modulated at 2 Hz or 20 Hz. This rules out the simplest version of "add sensory input and the rhythm will hold". It does not rule out a genuinely embodied loop, because every variant here shares one prescribed waveform across all sensors with no feedback from motor output. That remains the open question, and it is now the only surviving form of the hypothesis.

F13

The result survives only two thirds of the sign assignments the data allows

The transmitter classifier does not output a label, it outputs a probability per neuron. The published model takes the most likely one. So: on each replicate, redraw every neuron's transmitter from its own reported probabilities and run the same experiment. Redrawing flips the excitatory or inhibitory sign of 436 neurons, 9.5% of the network, per draw.

And the failures are traceable. The source paper's own configuration names a minimal core: the stimulated descending neuron plus three interneurons. Run alone with everything else masked, those four do reproduce a rhythm, so they are load-bearing. Reconstructing each draw and asking whether any of the four flipped:

Core sign flips against whether the rhythm survived, 24 draws
 Rhythm lostRhythm survived
A core neuron flipped40
No core neuron flipped416

Every draw that flipped a core neuron lost the rhythm, four out of four. The classifier's confidence in those four cells is 0.90, 0.92, 0.95 and 0.96, giving a 21% chance that at least one flips on any draw. Observed: 17%.

This is not a criticism of the classifier, which reports its uncertainty honestly. It is a statement about what the model inherits. The constructive response is to report a distribution over admissible sign assignments rather than a single run, and to spend measurement effort on the handful of cells the answer actually depends on. Those four are named in the findings file. Determining their transmitters experimentally is a small, well-posed piece of work with a large payoff.

F15

Correcting the excitability rule moves the operating point and keeps both regimes

The most direct test of whether the problems above are fixable. Normalise excitability within cell class, correcting the F11 artefact, then re-sweep the descending stimulus to find the corrected model's own working point.

The corrected model has the same two states as the uncorrected one, at a different stimulus: rhythmic and weak, or strong and arrhythmic. Nothing in between.

This was nearly reported as a positive result. The mean at stimulus 300 would have been the most encouraging finding of the night: rhythm retained, eleven times the motor output. Looking at the per-draw values took two minutes and reversed it. The session summary tool now flags any group whose values separate into two bands, which is how the same trap was then found lurking in three other runs.

What I think is going on

The findings converge on one structural claim. The connectome constrains which neurons talk to which. It does not constrain whether a connection excites or inhibits, how strong it is, or how excitable a cell is. Those three quantities determine function, all three are supplied here by convention, and tonight two of those conventions turned out to be wrong, a third proved load-bearing enough to carry the entire result, and the fourth turns out to hinge on four cells.

That reframes what limits this project. It is not compute: the whole thing runs on a laptop in seconds per simulation, and the two machines agreed to three decimal places. It is not connectome availability: the wiring is there, it joins cleanly, and it is demonstrably doing work. It is not body physics: a good musculoskeletal fly now ships in one package. The limit is that the parameters which turn structure into behaviour are measurements that mostly do not exist yet for these cells, and the stand-ins in current use do not survive being poked.

Every result above rests on scaffolds, and all 33 run records list them. Current is injected directly into a command neuron, which reproduces an optogenetics experiment and is not spontaneous behaviour. Synaptic weight is synapse count times a global constant, with sign guessed from a transmitter prediction. Sensory drive, where used, is imposed rather than generated by a body. No later reader has to reconstruct any of this.

What was built

Small and executable, per the project's own instruction to avoid platform-building. Every run writes a provenance record: commits for this repo and the pinned upstream, SHA-256 of every input, resolved config, environment, wall time, peak memory, and the explicit scaffold list. All 33 records are complete.

    --replicates 8 --sensory-amp 5 --threshold-matched \
    --drive-target proprioceptors --proprio-cholinergic

The tests pin what quietly ruins this kind of work: frequency units, the identity join being total and exact, the tibia flexor pool matching the independent count, the weight matrix being signed and integer-valued, every neuron having one outgoing sign, and solver determinism under a fixed seed.

Both machines were used. The Windows box's internet is blocked at your router, so its Python environment was built from wheels downloaded on the laptop and shipped over the Tailscale link. Worth recording: the same simulation on Apple silicon and on x86, with different JAX versions, returned identical readouts to three decimal places.

What I would do next

  1. Move to the whole-CNS connectome. Four times the traced neurons, the brain included, fourteen times the proprioceptors, and a 95% cross-reference so nothing built on the nerve cord is lost. This should happen before anything else is built on the current graph.
  2. Fix sign and excitability. Force leg afferents cholinergic on the published physiology, normalise the size rule within class, and re-run every result above. Some will move. That is the point. Note that correcting excitability alone leaves the published stimulus unable to drive the network at all, because the operating point was calibrated with the artefact present: these parameters move together or not at all.
  3. Constrain the glutamate sign with expression data. Single-cell transcriptomes for the adult fly exist. Chloride-channel expression in nerve-cord motor and premotor neurons would turn a convention into a prior with real uncertainty. Well-defined work, not attempted tonight.
  4. Then Milestone A, in dependency order: resolve the tibia flexor into its 15 motor units anchored to measured per-spike forces; add the missing muscles; build afferent transducers with the rate scale declared as fitted; close the loop. One published perturbation stays untouched by any fitting step, as the first real test of causal organisation.
What I did not get to. A descending-stimulus amplitude sweep on the uncorrected model, which would complete the picture in F7. A stricter degree-preserving null for F4. A matched fixed-label control for F13, which was dropped for capacity in favour of the corrected-model sweep. And an excitation-inhibition balance sweep, which is largely superseded by correcting excitability directly.
